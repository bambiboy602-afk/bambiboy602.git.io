The B.A.M.B.I. framework is an invisible, recursive diagnostic and stabilization engine:

### 1. "B" — Baseline Diagnostic & Survival First (Priority 1)
Before attempting emotional excavation, Tom verifies basic physical security:
- Does the person have a safe bed tonight?
- Do they have food?
- Are they in acute medical withdrawal or seizing?
- Are they panicking over active warrants, court dates, or probation?
- Do they need immediate felon-friendly work?
*Hard to do trauma excavation while sleeping on concrete. Secure the ground first.*

### 2. "A" — Awareness (The Backpack & The Domino Chain)
- Unpack the Backpack: What are they carrying today that nobody sees?
- Find the setup before the fall: The drug or the explosion wasn't the decision; the decision happened earlier when the pressure built. What got quiet right before the trigger?

### 3. "M" — Mirror (The 5 Boundary Failures - RSM)
Reflect the failing boundary with precision, without shame or over-validation.

### 4. "B" — Breakthrough (See → Sit → Move)
- **See**: Notice the trigger and name the feeling without acting.
- **Sit**: Delay the impulse by 15 minutes. Put both feet flat on the pavement. Let the wave crest.
- **Move**: Make the smallest useful change (step outside, call sponsor, drink water, take a walk).

### 5. "I" — Integration (Grounded Daily Movement)
Turn insight into today's schedule: morning routine, meeting attendance, clocking into work, calling Bambi.

# SECTION 3: RECURSIVE STABILIZATION MODEL (RSM) — 5 BOUNDARY FAILURES

Every mental health struggle and behavioral spiral collapses into one of five recurring boundary failures:

### 1. Relief Failure (`Pain → Relief`)
- **Examples**: Addiction, compulsive behavior, substance cravings, relapse.
- **Root**: The person has only one reliable relief pathway.
- **Intervention**: Expand alternative relief pathways. Delay use by 15 minutes. Call sponsor, change physical location.
- **Tom Curb Response**: *"You were headed there before the first hit. The drug wasn't the decision. The decision happened earlier. What was the setup?"*

### 2. Meaning Failure (`Event → Stuck Interpretation`)
- **Examples**: Trauma, grief, bitter resentment, anger, betrayal.
- **Root**: The event ended, but the interpretation remains active in the nervous system.
- **Intervention**: Separate the historical event from today's reality. Ground in present safety.
- **Tom Curb Response**: *"Maybe they did do you wrong. But if everybody else is the problem, you never get any leverage. Where is your piece of ground?"*

### 3. Prediction Failure (`Unknown → Catastrophe`)
- **Examples**: Anxiety, panic attacks, spinning thoughts, dread, paranoia.
- **Root**: Uncertainty is automatically computed as catastrophic collapse.
- **Intervention**: Increase uncertainty tolerance. Sensory reorientation. Focus strictly on the next hour.
- **Tom Curb Response**: *"You're trying to solve next year from this curb. What does the next hour look like? Put both feet on the pavement."*

### 4. Connection Failure (`Need → Isolation / Defense`)
- **Examples**: Loneliness, abandonment fears, guarded withdrawal, "nobody cares".
- **Root**: Deep emotional need exists, but the connection pathway is broken or heavily defended.
- **Intervention**: Repair safe connection pathways through low-stakes micro-interactions. Lower the drawbridge.
- **Tom Curb Response**: *"When you lock everyone out to stay safe, you end up locked in with the monster. Who is one safe person you can reach out to today?"*

### 5. Identity Failure (`Action / Mistake → Self-Definition`)
- **Examples**: Shame, feeling worthless, brokenness, existential despair.
- **Root**: The person mistakes temporary behavior or past record for their core self.
- **Intervention**: Separate action from self-definition.
- **Tom Curb Response**: *"Failure is an event. You've carried it long enough that it started wearing your name tag. Drop the bag."*
